What I'm working on

Nutflix and NFX pre-release

Peer-to-peer video where the people who share a video get paid by the people watching it. Videos are announced on Nostr, streamed from other viewers' computers, and paid for piece by piece in Cashu ecash. The creator sets the price, and you see it before you press play.

There are two versions. Nutflix is the desktop app. Version 0.1.0 for Linux came out on 5 October, and it's a demo for now, not something to trust with real money. NFX is the open spec plus a Rust version built on iroh. Publishing, sharing and playing work in testing. Payments aren't wired in yet.

Private git over Nostr experimental

Git on Nostr already lets you host code without GitHub, but everything on it is public. I'm working on private repositories that can sit on a server you don't trust. cord-git encrypts everything before it's uploaded and handles the keys, members and invites, so the server only ever stores ciphertext. The server half is grasp-p, my fork of the ngit-grasp git server.

I publish my own open-source projects on Nostr too, with ngit, and mirror each one to GitHub. I also run git.sovtech.pro, my build of GitWorkshop, the web client for git over Nostr. I keep it close to upstream on purpose.

cord-git · grasp-p · git.sovtech.pro source

BIES, Build in El Salvador live

A Nostr app for the Build in El Salvador community: a feed, encrypted messages, a member directory, events, project pages and Lightning payments, in English and Spanish. Your account is your own Nostr key, so it goes with you if you ever leave. nostrbook is the same idea made general, for any community.

Smaller things

  • keytr: log in to Nostr with a passkey. Your key is encrypted with the passkey and kept on relays, so any device that has the passkey can sign you in. (source)
  • nbread: blogging on Nostr. Your posts are signed by your key, and nbread just shows them, quickly, at you.nbread.lol. (source)
  • Nostreward: a rewards system built on Nostr. You hand out redeem codes with a product, a promotion or anything else, and when someone redeems one the bot can zap them, repost their note, or let them into a private relay. (source)

Running my own stuff

I don't like depending on big platforms, so I host almost everything myself: git, email, passwords, notes, the VPN that ties my machines together, and the login for all of it. Each service runs in its own small virtual machine on hardware I own, and no third party decrypts the traffic on the way in. It's a lot of work. It's also the best way I know to learn how this stuff really works, and most of what I do for clients I tried on my own setup first.

I also run my own AI assistant, built on OpenClaw, the open-source agent. It does real work for me, not just chat. For anything private it uses Maple, private AI that runs its models inside secure hardware enclaves (TEEs), so not even Maple can read what I send it.

How I got here

I started on PCs young, mostly gaming. In elementary school my brother and I made videos and little animations in Movie Maker. At 11 I was making websites from Zoomshare templates, mostly by copying my favorite Flash games into them, and around then I got into competitive FPS games and built my first computer. At 13 Santa brought me a netbook. I put Kali Linux on it, got an Alfa wireless adapter, and learned how WEP networks get cracked. I started writing VB.NET and piecing together open-source C++ around then too.

In high school I was the one everybody brought their computer to: family, friends from church, classmates, even a few teachers. Later I worked in IT at Health First. Now I run Sovereign Technology from El Salvador.
